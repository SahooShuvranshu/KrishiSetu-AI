## 🌾 1. Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders of the **KrishiSetu AI** open-source initiative pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free and empowering experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, spoken language, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community dedicated to engineering digital public goods for smallholder farmers.

---

## 🚜 2. Agricultural Ethos & Cultural Respect

KrishiSetu AI is not a generic software project; it is a **Digital Public Good** designed to safeguard the livelihood of smallholder farmers across Odisha and India who operate in resource-constrained environments:

- **Empathy for the End User**: Always remember that our software is used by real farmers in remote fields under harsh sunlight, often facing crop failure or literacy barriers. Technical discussions, UI decisions, and diagnostic features must prioritize simplicity, accessibility, and respect for rural wisdom.
- **Multilingual Dignity**: We deeply respect linguistic diversity. Derogatory remarks, mockery, or elitist attitudes regarding translations, colloquial expressions, non-English contributions (Odia `ଓଡ଼ିଆ`, Hindi `हिन्दी`), or grammatical variations in English are strictly prohibited.
- **Scientific Integrity**: Because incorrect chemical dosages can destroy family crops or cause chemical poisoning, we hold discussions surrounding agronomy, pathology datasets, and AI diagnostic thresholds to the highest standard of factual accuracy and scientific honesty.

## 📋 3. Our Standards

### ✅ Positive Behaviors
Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive, thriving environment include:
* **Demonstrating Empathy**: Treating every contributor, community member, and field user with kindness and patience.
* **Respectful Discourse**: Being respectful of differing technical opinions, architectural perspectives, and agronomic approaches.
* **Graceful Feedback**: Providing constructive, actionable feedback on code and pull requests, and accepting feedback gracefully without hostility.
* **Accountability & Apology**: Acknowledging mistakes promptly, apologizing sincerely to those affected, and proactively learning from errors.
* **Community-First Focus**: Centering engineering decisions around what delivers the greatest benefit to smallholder farmers and community welfare.
* **Welcoming Beginners**: Mentoring newcomers, clarifying complex ML concepts, and supporting first-time open-source contributors.

### 🚫 Unacceptable Behaviors
Examples of unacceptable behavior include:
* **Harassment & Discrimination**: The use of sexualized language or imagery, unwelcome sexual attention, or discrimination based on identity.
* **Trolling & Hostility**: Trolling, insultingly dismissive code review comments, derogatory remarks, or personal/political attacks.
* **Doxxing & Privacy Invasions**: Publishing others' private personal information, phone numbers, physical addresses, or contact information without explicit permission.
* **Agronomic Misinformation**: Deliberately inserting unverified, hazardous, or toxic chemical dosage advice into diagnosis files.
* **Gatekeeping & Elitism**: Condescending language regarding an individual's technical background, hardware limitations, or linguistic proficiency.
* **Disruptive Conduct**: Sustained disruption of issues, discussions, or pull requests that obstruct project development.

## 🛡️ 4. Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders (**Crystal Studio Labs**) are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior. They are empowered to take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to edit, remove, or reject comments, commits, code, pull requests, issues, and other contributions that do not align with this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## ⚖️ 6. Enforcement Resolution Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action deemed in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### Level 1: Correction
* **Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.
* **Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### Level 2: Warning
* **Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions demonstrating disrespect, dismissiveness, or mild harassment.
* **Consequence**: A formal warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time.

### Level 3: Temporary Suspension
* **Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment, or malicious code insertion.
* **Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time (e.g., 30 days). Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### Level 4: Permanent Ban
* **Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation, including sustained harassment, doxxing, hate speech, sabotage of agricultural datasets, or refusal to respect community leadership.
* **Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the project repositories, organizations, and communication channels.

## 🌐 7. Scope of Application

This Code of Conduct applies within all official KrishiSetu AI project spaces (including the GitHub organization, repository issues, discussions, pull requests, project boards, and Discord/Slack channels), and it also applies when an individual is officially representing the project in public spaces.

Examples of representing our project include using an official project e-mail address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event (such as the Google AI Hackathon 2026).
